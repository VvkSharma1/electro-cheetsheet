1.3 Fundamentals of Waves

1-D wave eqn: \(\dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial x^2}-\dfrac{1}{c_s^2}\dfrac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2}=0\). Traveling-wave solution:

\[ p(x,t)=A_m\cos(\omega t-\beta x+\psi) \]

\(\beta\)(rad/m)=phase/wavenumber; \(\omega=2\pi f\).

\[ \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{\beta},\qquad v_p=\lambda f=\frac{\omega}{\beta} \]

Sign rule: phase \(\omega t-\beta x\) (decreasing with \(x\)) \(\Rightarrow\) \(+x\) travel; \(\omega t+\beta x\Rightarrow -x\) travel.

Trap: Mixing up \(+\beta x\) vs \(-\beta x\) direction sign.

1.5 Phasors

\(A(t)=A_m\cos(\omega t+\psi) \leftrightarrow C=A_m e^{j\psi}\) (freq. suppressed).

\[ A(t)=\mathrm{Re}\{Ce^{j\omega t}\} \]

Enables: peak \(=|C|\), phase \(=\angle C\) (no search/integration); derivatives \(\to \times j\omega\).

Use: Any sinusoidal-steady-state (AC) analysis — circuits, TL, plane waves.

Trap: Phasor magnitude (\(A_m\), peak) vs. rms \(=A_m/\sqrt2\).

2.2–2.7 E, D, B, H

Field Quantity Definitions
E (V/m): force/charge, \(\mathbf{E}=\lim_{q\to0}\mathbf F/q\); points where \(V\) drops fastest, mag.=rate of drop.
D (C/m\(^2\)) \(=\epsilon \mathbf E\): flux description; \(\oint\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf s=Q_{encl}\) (Gauss).
B (T=Wb/m\(^2\)): \(\mathbf F=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B\); field lines always close.
H (A/m) \(=\mathbf B/\mu\): field with material effect factored out.

Constitutive: \(\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E\), \(\mathbf B=\mu\mathbf H\), \(\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E\). \(\epsilon_0\approx8.854\times10^{-12}\)F/m, \(\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\)H/m, \(\epsilon_r=\epsilon/\epsilon_0\), \(\mu_r=\mu/\mu_0\) (typ. \(\approx1\) except ferromagnets \(\sim10^6\)).

Use: \(D\) matters at boundaries (perp. component constrained); \(H\) matters at boundaries (tangential constrained). In homogeneous media \(D,B\) look redundant with \(E,H\).

Cond: LTI (homogeneous, isotropic, linear, time-invariant) needed for superposition to hold.

4.5–4.10 Vector Operators

\[ \nabla f=\hat x\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+\hat y\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}+\hat z\frac{\partial f}{\partial z} \] \[ \nabla\cdot\mathbf A=\partial_x A_x+\partial_y A_y+\partial_z A_z \] \[ \nabla\times\mathbf A=\hat x(\partial_y A_z-\partial_z A_y)+\hat y(\partial_z A_x-\partial_x A_z)+\hat z(\partial_x A_y-\partial_y A_x) \] \[ \nabla^2 f=\nabla\cdot(\nabla f)=\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial y^2}+\frac{\partial^2f}{\partial z^2} \]

Cylindrical \(\nabla=\hat\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\hat\phi\frac1\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\hat z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\). Spherical \(\nabla=\hat r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\hat\theta\frac1r\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\hat\phi\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\).

Divergence Thm / Stokes' Thm
\[ \int_{\mathcal V}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)\,dv=\oint_{\mathcal S}\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf s \] \[ \int_{\mathcal S}(\nabla\times\mathbf A)\cdot d\mathbf s=\oint_{\mathcal C}\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf l \] Converts volume↔surface (Div. Thm) or surface↔closed-path (Stokes) integrals.
When to use which operator
Grad→ rate/dir. of max increase of scalar (\(E=-\nabla V\)). Div→ flux/source density per vol. (Gauss' law, \(\rho_v\)). Curl→ circulation per area (Faraday, Ampere). Div.Thm→ swap vol.↔closed-surf. integral. Stokes→ swap open-surf.↔closed-path integral. Laplacian→ 2nd-derivative/Poisson's eqn.

Trap: \(\nabla\times\mathbf A\) determinant sign errors; forgetting \(1/\rho\), \(1/(r\sin\theta)\) metric factors in curvilinear coords.

Material Properties & Constitutive Params
\(\epsilon\)(F/m): \(E\) response to charge. \(\mu\)(H/m): \(B\) response to current. \(\sigma\)(S/m): \(J\) response to \(E\) (\(J=\sigma E\)). Frequency dependence: good conductor (\(\sigma\to\infty\)): conduction current \(J=\sigma E\) dominates, \(E\to0\) inside, no energy storage (\(\epsilon\) irrelevant). Lossless dielectric (\(\sigma\approx0\)): displacement current dominates, characterized by \(\epsilon\) alone. Ranges: insulators \(\sigma\ll10^{-10}\); semicond. \(10^{-4}\)–\(10^{1}\); conductors \(>10^{5}\)S/m. Perfect conductor: \(\sigma\to\infty\Rightarrow E\to0\), \(V=\)const (equipotential vol.).

2.8 Linearity & Reciprocity

Homogeneous: params constant in space. Isotropic: same in all orientations. Linear: params independent of field magnitude. Time-invariant: params don't change in time. Consequence: LTI \(\Rightarrow\) superposition valid: \(E_{tot}=E_1+E_2\) for independent sources. Reciprocity: response unchanged if source & observation points are swapped (used for antenna Tx/Rx equivalence).

Power & Energy: Circuit ↔ Field ↔ Wave

Circuit: \(P=IV\). Field (Joule heating): \(P=\int_{\mathcal V}\sigma|E|^2\,dv=\int\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf J\,dv\) (reduces to \(P=IV\) for uniform wire). Wave: instantaneous \(\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\) (Poynting, W/m\(^2\)); time-avg \(S_{ave}=\frac12\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf E}\times\tilde{\mathbf H}^*\}\). TL: \(P_{av}^+=|V_0^+|^2/2Z_0\). Conservation: \(P_{inc}=P_{refl}+P_{load}\).

Use: Field power → heat generation in lossy media; wave power → radiated/transmitted energy flow.

USRP / Spectrum Scanning (conceptual)

A spectrum analyzer/USRP sweeps a local-oscillator frequency, mixing the received signal down and measuring power in narrow bands → plots power vs. frequency. Peaks = occupied channels/signals; noise floor = receiver+environment noise; occupied bandwidth = span of a peak above floor; center frequency = peak location. Spectrum allocation chart: regulatory division of frequency bands among services (broadcast, cellular, satellite, radar,…) to prevent interference — motivates band planning in Table above.

Transmission Lines & Plane Waves

3.4–3.9 TL Model & Wave Eqn

Distributed per-length: \(R'(\Omega/m)\) series loss, \(L'(H/m)\) series ind., \(G'(S/m)\) shunt leak, \(C'(F/m)\) shunt cap. Telegrapher's Eqns (phasor):

\[ -\frac{\partial \tilde V}{\partial z}=(R'+j\omega L')\tilde I,\qquad -\frac{\partial \tilde I}{\partial z}=(G'+j\omega C')\tilde V \]

Wave eqn: \(\dfrac{\partial^2\tilde V}{\partial z^2}-\gamma^2\tilde V=0\), \quad \(\gamma=\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')(G'+j\omega C')}=\alpha+j\beta\).

\[ \tilde V(z)=V_0^+e^{-\gamma z}+V_0^-e^{+\gamma z} \]

\(\alpha\)(Np/m)=attenuation (Re\(\{\gamma\}\)); \(\beta\)(rad/m)=phase const. (Im\(\{\gamma\}\)).

\[ Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{R'+j\omega L'}{G'+j\omega C'}}=\frac{V_0^+}{I_0^+}=\frac{-V_0^-}{I_0^-} \]

Use: \(Z_0\) relates co-propagating \(V,I\) only (not total \(V/I\)).

Lossless / Low-loss
Lossless (\(R'=G'=0\)) or low-loss (\(R'\ll\omega L'\), \(G'\ll\omega C'\)): \[ \alpha\approx0,\quad \beta\approx\omega\sqrt{L'C'},\quad v_p=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L'C'}} \] \[ Z_0\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}\ \ (\text{real}) \]

Cond: Practical lines are usually low-loss; use exact \(\gamma,Z_0\) only if \(R',G'\) significant.

Coax: \(C'=\dfrac{2\pi\epsilon_s}{\ln(b/a)}\), \(L'=\dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\ln\dfrac{b}{a}\), \(Z_0\approx\dfrac{60\Omega}{\sqrt{\epsilon_r}}\ln\dfrac{b}{a}\), \(v_p\approx c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\).

Trap: \(R'\) rises with \(\sqrt f\) (skin effect) — not constant.

3.12–3.14 \(\Gamma\), Standing Waves, SWR

\[ \Gamma\triangleq\frac{Z_L-Z_0}{Z_L+Z_0},\qquad 0\le|\Gamma|\le1 \]
Special cases of \(\Gamma\)
\(Z_L=Z_0\)(matched)\(\Gamma=0\)
\(Z_L\to\infty\)(open)\(\Gamma=+1\)
\(Z_L=0\)(short)\(\Gamma=-1\)
\(Z_L=jX\)(react.)\(|\Gamma|=1\), phase \(=\pi-2\phi\)
\[ \tilde V(z)=V_0^+\big(e^{-j\beta z}+\Gamma e^{+j\beta z}\big) \] \[ |\tilde V(z)|=|V_0^+|\sqrt{1+|\Gamma|^2+2|\Gamma|\cos(2\beta z+\phi)} \]

Standing-wave period \(=\lambda/2\); \(V\) max ↔ \(I\) min and vice-versa.

\[ \mathrm{SWR}=\frac{1+|\Gamma|}{1-|\Gamma|}\in[1,\infty),\qquad |\Gamma|=\frac{\mathrm{SWR}-1}{\mathrm{SWR}+1} \]

Use: \(\Gamma\): given \(Z_L,Z_0\). SWR: given \(|\Gamma|\) (match quality; SWR\(<\)2 typically "good").

Trap: VSWR = current SWR always (not separate).

\[ Z_{in}(l)=Z_0\,\frac{1+\Gamma e^{-j2\beta l}}{1-\Gamma e^{-j2\beta l}}=Z_0\,\frac{Z_L+jZ_0\tan(\beta l)}{Z_0+jZ_L\tan(\beta l)} \]

Periodic in \(l\) with period \(\lambda/2\). \(\beta l\)=electrical length.

Short (\(Z_L=0\)): \(Z_{in}=+jZ_0\tan\beta l\)
Open (\(Z_L\to\infty\)): \(Z_{in}=-jZ_0\cot\beta l\)
Alternates open↔short every \(\lambda/4\); purely reactive (inductor if \(\tan>0\), capacitor if \(<0\)).

Use: \(Z_{in}\): given \(Z_L,Z_0,l\). Stub \(Z_{in}\): replace discrete \(L\)/\(C\) with transmission-line stub.

3.19–3.20 Quarter-Wave & Power Flow

Quarter-wave transformer: for \(l=\lambda/4\) match of \(Z_L\) to source \(Z_{in}\):

\[ Z_0=\sqrt{Z_{in}\cdot Z_L} \]

Cond: Requires real-valued \(Z_L\) (or pre-transform to real with a short line first).

Power Flow on TL
\[ P_{av}^+=\frac{|V_0^+|^2}{2Z_0},\qquad P_{av}^-=|\Gamma|^2P_{av}^+ \] \[ P_L=(1-|\Gamma|^2)\,P_{av}^+ \quad\text{(conservation: }P^+=P^-+P_L) \]

Use: Fraction reflected \(=|\Gamma|^2\); fraction delivered \(=1-|\Gamma|^2\).

3.21–3.23 Impedance Matching

Why: standardize \(Z_0\) (50/75\(\Omega\)), antenna \(Z\) varies w/ freq, amplifier design needs specific \(Z\).

Single-Reactance Matching
Series: choose \(l\) so \(\mathrm{Re}\{Z_1(l)\}=\mathrm{Re}\{Z_{in}\}\) (solve \(Z_1=Z_0\frac{1+\Gamma e^{-j2\beta l}}{1-\Gamma e^{-j2\beta l}}\)); then \(X_s=\mathrm{Im}\{Z_{in}-Z_1\}\) in series (\(X_s>0\) ind., \(<0\) cap.).
Parallel (admittance): \(\mathrm{Re}\{Y_1\}=\mathrm{Re}\{Y_{in}\}\), then \(B_p=\mathrm{Im}\{Y_{in}-Y_1\}\) shunt.
Single-Stub Matching
Main line length \(l_1\): solve \(\mathrm{Re}\{Y_1(l_1)\}=\mathrm{Re}\{Y_{in}\}\). Stub (length \(l_2\), same or diff. \(Z_{02}\)) cancels \(\mathrm{Im}\{Y_1\}\): \[ B_p=+Y_{02}\tan\beta_2l_2\ (\text{open}) \] \[ B_p=-Y_{02}\cot\beta_2l_2\ (\text{short}) \] Choose open/short giving shorter stub (unless DC-block/pass needed).

Mini-template: (1) find \(\Gamma\) (2) find \(l_1\) real-part match (3) find reactance/stub to cancel imag. part (4) verify \(Z_{in}=\)target.

Trap: Quarter-wave zeros the imaginary part first (different order than single-reactance method).

9.1–9.2 Maxwell Phasor & Wave Eqns

Source-free, lossless, homog., isotropic:

\[ \nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf E}=0,\quad \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf E}=-j\omega\mu\tilde{\mathbf H} \] \[ \nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf H}=0,\quad \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf H}=+j\omega\epsilon\tilde{\mathbf E} \] \[ \nabla^2\tilde{\mathbf E}+\beta^2\tilde{\mathbf E}=0,\qquad \beta=\omega\sqrt{\mu\epsilon} \]

Cond: \(\sigma=0\) required (source-free precludes conduction loss); complex \(\epsilon\) needed if lossy.

9.4–9.5 Uniform Plane Waves

Uniform over a plane \(\Rightarrow\) \(\mathbf E,\mathbf H\) lie in that plane and propagation \(\perp\) to it; \(\mathbf E,\mathbf H,\hat k\) mutually \(\perp\), with \(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H \parallel \hat k\) (propagation dir.).

\[ \tilde{\mathbf E}=\hat x E_0e^{-j\beta z},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf H}=\hat y\frac{E_0}{\eta}e^{-j\beta z} \] \[ \eta\triangleq\frac{\tilde E_x}{\tilde H_y}=\sqrt{\frac\mu\epsilon}\ (\Omega),\qquad \eta_0=\sqrt{\mu_0/\epsilon_0}\approx377\,\Omega \] \[ v_p=\frac{\omega}{\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu\epsilon}},\qquad \lambda=\frac{2\pi}{\beta}=\frac{v_p}{f} \]

Plane-wave relations: \(\tilde{\mathbf H}=\dfrac{1}{\eta}\hat k\times\tilde{\mathbf E}\), \(\tilde{\mathbf E}=-\eta\,\hat k\times\tilde{\mathbf H}\).

Use: Given \(\mathbf E\) + prop. dir. \(\hat k\) → find \(\mathbf H\) (and vice versa) via right-hand relation.

Trap: \(v_p,\lambda\) shrink vs. free space by \(1/\sqrt{\mu_r\epsilon_r}\); \(\eta\) changes with medium, not just \(\beta\).

9.6 Polarization

\[ \tilde{\mathbf E}=(\hat x E_x+\hat y E_y)e^{-j\beta z} \]
Polarization Cases
Linear\(E_x,E_y\) same phase (or one \(=0\))
Circular\(|E_x|=|E_y|\), phase diff. \(=\pm\pi/2\) (LCP/RCP)

9.7 Wave Power

\[ \mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\ (\text{W/m}^2,\text{ instantaneous, Poynting vector}) \] \[ S_{ave}=\frac12\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf E}\times\tilde{\mathbf H}^*\}=\hat z\,\frac{|E_0|^2}{2\eta} \]

Use: Time-avg power density of a plane wave, given \(|E_0|\) (peak) and medium \(\eta\).

Trap: Peak vs. rms \(E_0\): rms form has no factor of 2 (\(S_{ave}=|E_{0,rms}|^2/\eta\)).

6.1–6.6 Steady Current & Conductivity

Ohm's Law for EM: \(\mathbf J=\sigma\mathbf E\) (S/m) — conduction current density response to \(E\).

\[ I=\int_S\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf s,\qquad R=\frac{l}{\sigma A},\qquad G=\frac1R=\frac{\sigma A}{l} \]

Coax conductance: \(G'=\dfrac{2\pi\sigma_s}{\ln(b/a)}\) (per length).

Power Dissipation (Joule heating)
\[ P=\int_{\mathcal V}\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf J\,dv=\int_{\mathcal V}\sigma|\mathbf E|^2\,dv=IV \] Perfect conductor (\(\sigma\to\infty\)) dissipates no power, since \(E\to0\) inside it regardless of current.

Use: \(R\): given \(\sigma,l,A\) of a uniform conductor. \(P\): given \(E\) (or \(\sigma,J\)) over a lossy volume.

Trap: Resistance \(\ne\) Re\(\{Z\}\) in general — e.g. a lossless \(Z_{in}\) can have nonzero real part.

8.9 Displacement Current & Ampere's Law

DC Ampere's law \(\oint\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l=I_{encl}\) fails for AC through a capacitor gap: surface \(S_1\) (cutting the wire) gives \(I_{encl}=I\), but \(S_2\) (through the gap, same boundary \(C\)) gives \(I_{encl}=0\) — contradiction unless a new term is added.

\[ I_d=\int_S\frac{\partial\mathbf D}{\partial t}\cdot d\mathbf s \quad(\text{displacement current}) \] \[ \oint_C\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l=I_c+I_d \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J+\frac{\partial\mathbf D}{\partial t} \]

Cond: \(I_d\) is zero for DC, nonzero only when \(E\) (hence \(D\)) is time-varying; it is not a conduction current, but has units of A and enters Ampere's law identically.

Use: Explains how \(H\) circulates around a capacitor gap with no conduction current — the "missing" current in the time-varying case.

Trap: Displacement current \(\ne\) actual charge motion between capacitor plates — it's \(\partial D/\partial t\), not \(J\).
